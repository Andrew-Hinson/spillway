# M3.5: cold path to object storage

2026-10-10 · local kind cluster from `make up` · Vector 0.58.0 · versitygw v1.8.0 · [ADR 0006](../adr/0006-versitygw-for-local-cold-storage.md)

Does a team routed cold get its complete redacted stream in object storage, partitioned by team and date and compressed, with no PII?

## Setup

`make up` now installs an S3-compatible store (versitygw, `make storage`) with a `spillway-cold` bucket and generated credentials, and the operator runs with `--cold-bucket`. Three cold-routed pipelines, applied together, were Ready through the canary in 2 m 11 s. Two of them, the `content` and `payments` examples, were `Invalid` until now.

| Team | Source | Policy | Routing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `content` | Wikimedia topic, ~37 events/s | budget 100/s | hot and cold |
| `payments` | pod logs from a generator: each second 4 debug and 2 info as JSON, 2 info as logfmt, 2 warn as klog | `debug: 0, info: 25`, budget 500/s | hot and cold |
| `fixtures-team` | the fixture injector's pod logs, 4/s | defaults | hot and cold |

`make fixtures RATE=4` ran throughout.

## Cold keeps everything, hot is thinned

Events logged 21:13–21:25 UTC, counted in the synced bucket and in Loki:

| Team | Level or type | Cold | Hot (Loki) | Hot / cold |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `content` | `categorize` | 11,621 | 11,621 | 100.0% |
| `content` | `edit` | 12,895 | 12,895 | 100.0% |
| `content` | `log` | 1,602 | 1,602 | 100.0% |
| `content` | `new` | 294 | 294 | 100.0% |
| `fixtures-team` | `warn` | 2,880 | 2,880 | 100.0% |
| `payments` | `debug` | 2,876 | 0 | 0.0% |
| `payments` | `info` | 2,876 | 758 | 26.4% |
| `payments` | `warn` | 1,438 | 1,438 | 100.0% |

- **`content`** has no sampling and a budget above its load, so cold and hot match type for type. Its 26,412 cold events carry 26,412 distinct event IDs: no duplicates.
- **`payments`**' cold copy has every debug and info line, which sampling removed from the hot path. The generator wrote ~2,880 lines per 4/s stream in 12 minutes. It wrote 719 seconds' worth, because the loop takes slightly over a second.
- **`fixtures-team`**: 2,880 = 4/s × 720 s.

## Partitioned, compressed, no PII

`make cold-check` syncs the bucket and checks every object. The key must be `team=<team>/date=<YYYY-MM-DD>/…log.gz`, the object must be gzip, and every line must be a JSON event whose `spillway.team` and log date match the key. It also searches every line for fixture values:

| Team | Date | Objects | Events | Fixtures | Event bytes | Stored (gzip) | Ratio |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `content` | 2026-10-10 | 34 | 39,177 | 4,324 | 54,464,666 | 7,825,976 | 14.4% |
| `fixtures-team` | 2026-10-10 | 33 | 3,992 | 3,992 | 2,116,248 | 181,089 | 8.6% |
| `payments` | 2026-10-10 | 31 | 9,710 | 0 | 2,722,926 | 58,440 | 2.1% |

**PASS: 52,879 events in 98 objects, 8,316 of them fixtures, 0 values leaked, no mismatched objects.** Gzip stores Wikimedia events at 14% of their size, and the repetitive generator lines at 2%.

The hot sink over the same period (`make leak-check WINDOW=12m`): 5,752 fixtures delivered, 0 values leaked.

The checker can fail: given a planted object with a phone fixture value, an event from another team, and a key outside `team=…/date=…/`, it reported the leak and both problems and exited 1.

## Found along the way: the agent ran out of memory

The first run of this test found fixture pod logs stopping at 20:12. The agent on the injector's node had been OOM-killed into a crash loop. Since #49 sends pod logs through Kafka, librdkafka's default queue took the agent past its 256 MiB limit. #51 fixed it (capped queue, lz4, a 512 MiB limit), and this run is on that fix. Its PR has the memory measurements.

## Limitations

- **Batches of up to 60 s or 10 MiB:** the newest minute of cold data is still in the aggregators' buffers. The canary's bake (2 min) is at least twice that timeout, as the operator requires.
- **Partitioned by log time, not arrival time:** a late event lands in its own day's prefix.
- **The local store is a gateway on one volume**, with no replication. In the cloud the sink writes to S3 (M4.3).
