# M3.4: per-team budgets

2026-10-10 · local kind cluster from `make up` · two aggregators · Vector 0.58.0 · [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-budgets-split-across-aggregators.md)

Does a team's `maxEventsPerSec` hold, and are the events over it counted?

## Setup

Two teams, each with `budget: {maxEventsPerSec: 10}` and no sampling:

| Team | Source | Offered load |
|---|---|---|
| `capped` | Wikimedia topic (3 partitions, split 2:1 across the aggregators) | ~25 events/s |
| `chatty` | pod logs from 2 generator pods on different nodes, 20 lines/s each, written in bursts once a second | 40 events/s |

Rates are `rate(...[5m])` of each team's `<team>_budget` counters, per aggregator pod. Three runs:
- **Before** is the M3.3 renderer: each pod throttles the whole budget over a 1 s window.
- **Split** is an even share per pod over a 10 s window, on the same cluster once the new operator had canaried it in. Pod logs still went straight from the agents to the aggregators.
- **Kafka** is the final change: the split, with pod logs through the `spillway.pods` topic. It was measured on a fresh `make up`.

## Results

| Team | Pod | Before: in | Before: out | Split: in | Split: out | Kafka: in | Kafka: out |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `capped` | aggregator-0 | 16.6/s | 9.6/s | 8.0/s | 4.8/s | 25.0/s | 5.0/s |
| `capped` | aggregator-1 | 8.3/s | 8.0/s | 16.9/s | 5.0/s | 12.0/s | 4.8/s |
| **`capped`** | **both** | 24.9/s | **17.5/s** | 24.9/s | **9.8/s** | 36.9/s | **9.8/s** |
| `chatty` | aggregator-0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 21.1/s | 5.0/s |
| `chatty` | aggregator-1 | 39.9/s | 7.9/s | 40.0/s | 5.0/s | 18.5/s | 4.8/s |
| **`chatty`** | **both** | 39.9/s | **7.9/s** | 40.0/s | **5.0/s** | 39.5/s | **9.8/s** |

Wikimedia's rate varies over the day: it was about 25/s for the first two runs and 37/s for the third.

Every event the throttle didn't pass was counted in `vector_component_discarded_events_total{component_id="<team>_budget", intentional="true", team="<team>"}`. Over-budget drops per second equal in minus out in every row above, and they appear per team in the dashboard's Teams row and in `make sampling-check`.

## What the numbers show

1. **Each aggregator enforced the whole budget.** There are two, so `capped` got 17.5/s against a budget of 10. Its partitions moved between pods when the new config rolled out, which is why the per-pod split differs between columns.
2. **A 1 s window under-delivers on batched input.** `chatty`'s aggregator passed 7.9/s, not 10. Events arrive in batches (agent batches, Kafka fetches), and a throttle releases at most one window's threshold at once, so any gap longer than the window wastes budget. Reproduced locally with the real binary: batches 1–2.5 s apart at 40/s gave **6.2/s** through a 10-per-1 s throttle, and **9.7/s** steady through 100 per 10 s, plus a 100-event burst at start. The same binary passes exactly 10/s with smooth input.
3. **Split:** each pod allows `floor(10 / 2) = 5`/s over a 10 s window. `capped`, spread over both pods, gets 9.8/s. `chatty`'s logs all reached one aggregator (each agent held one long-lived gRPC connection, and all of them went to aggregator-1), so it got that pod's share: **5.0/s, half its budget.**
4. **Kafka:** with pod logs written to `spillway.pods` (6 partitions, split 3:3 by the consumer group), `chatty`'s 40/s reached the aggregators as 21.1/s and 18.5/s, and it got **9.8/s**. Consumer lag on the topic stayed under 100 events per partition. Loki's entry time for a pod log matched the line's own timestamp (a klog line at 18:54:14.329 was stored at 18:54:14.329), so the log time survives the JSON round trip through Kafka.

## Pod logs through Kafka leak nothing

Moving pod logs to Kafka changed the path every pod-log fixture takes, so the leak check ran again on the Kafka run, with `make fixtures RATE=4` and a team claiming the `fixtures` namespace. `make leak-check WINDOW=10m`:

| Stream | Fixtures delivered | Delivered without a mask | Fixture values found |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `team=capped` | 1,062 | 0 | 0 |
| `team=fixtures-team` | 2,397 | 0 | 0 |
| **total** | **3,459** | **0** | **0** |

`fixtures-team` got 2,397 of the 2,400 pod-log fixtures; the other 3 were in flight at the window's edge. `capped` got 1,062 of 2,400 Kafka fixtures, because its 10/s budget dropped the rest along with other events.

## Limitations

- **An even split needs an even spread.** A team whose events reach the aggregators unevenly gets less than its budget. With pod logs on a 6-partition topic that's no longer a problem in practice, but the 3-partition Wikimedia topic splits 2:1 on two aggregators. See ADR 0005.
- **Bursts after idle:** a team quiet for 10 s can send up to 10 s of its share at once.
- **Rounding down:** 25/s on two pods is 12 + 12 = 24/s. A budget smaller than the number of pods becomes 1/s per pod.
- **Status didn't show the re-render.** When the new operator changed the budget shares, the pipelines stayed `RolledOut` through the canary: status tracks spec generations, and no spec changed.
