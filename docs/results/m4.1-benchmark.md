# M4.1 benchmark: throughput and latency under stepped load

The benchmark harness from the [build plan](../plan.md) (M4, issue 1: *stepped load; throughput and p99 end-to-end latency written to `docs/results`*), run against a fresh `make up` with `make bench` ([`bench/m4_bench.sh`](../../bench/m4_bench.sh)).

**Result: 32,000 events/s sustained, with p99 latency of 29 ms up to 3,000 events/s.** Every step through 40,000 events/s was delivered completely: 35,520,000 events, each found in Loki once, none missing and none duplicated.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| **Sustained throughput** (highest step with stable buffers and no drops) | **32,000 events/s**: two aggregators, 4.7 cores between them, 704 and 759 MiB |
| **p99 latency**, feeder produce → aggregator pre-sink | **29 ms up to 3,000 events/s** (p50 12–13 ms), the M1 baseline's figure. 544 ms at 4,000/s, about 1 s at 6,000–8,000/s, **3.7 s at 32,000/s** |
| Events through 40,000/s (11 steps, 35.5 M events) | **0 missing, 0 duplicated**, counted in Loki per step against what Kafka acknowledged |
| What stops it | 40,000/s: Loki's ingestion limit (64 MB/s, as configured here) throttled the hot sink; Vector retried, and every event arrived. 48,000/s: **both aggregators were OOM-killed** (1 GiB limit). They restarted and re-read from their last offset commit: 143,199 duplicates, no loss visible in the counts |

The plan's two numbers pull apart: the pipeline sustains ten times the rate at which its latency stays near the baseline. Up to 3,000 events/s, latency is the producer's linger and Kafka. Above it, queues build through the team's transforms ([below](#latency-the-knee)), and p99 rises to seconds while throughput keeps up.

## Method

**Sustained throughput**, as the plan defines it: the highest step with stable buffers and no drops. `bench/throughput.py` measures each step over its last 3 minutes, after 1 minute of warm-up, and calls it sustained when:

- the generator sent at least 98% of the target (otherwise the generator or Kafka was the limit),
- the aggregators consumed at least 95% of what was produced (counters are scraped every 15 s, so per-step rates carry a few percent of noise; backlog is the strict test),
- Kafka consumer lag grew by no more than 5 s of traffic over the step, and both sink buffers by no more than 2 s, by a linear fit,
- nothing was discarded, no component reported an error, and neither sink had a request rejected.

The 5 s lag slack is Vector's Kafka commit interval: lag is measured against committed offsets, so it saws between 0 and 5 s of traffic even when the aggregators keep up. At 1,000 events/s the mean lag was 1.2 s of traffic while the p50 latency was 12 ms.

**Latency**, as in the M1 baseline: `spillway.sink_at − spillway.produced_at`, from the generator handing an event to its Kafka producer to the team's last transform before the hot sink (`bench_hot`). It includes the producer's 20 ms linger, Kafka, and every transform; it doesn't include the sink's batching (up to 1 s) or Loki's write. Quantiles are interpolated within the histogram's buckets (…, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150, 250, 500, 1000, 2500 ms, …).

**End-to-end counts**: once the backlog has drained, the report counts every event at each stage, from 30 s before the first step to the end of the drain, from the raw samples of Vector's counters (exact unless a pod is killed: its last increments are never scraped). It also counts the lines Loki holds for `{team="bench"}`, over the whole run and per step: each step's events carry Kafka timestamps from inside it, so Loki's count in each step's window is matched to the events Kafka acknowledged for that step.

Notes on the tables:

- **40,000/s** wasn't sustained only because Loki answered 129 pushes with 429 (rate limited): 40,000 events/s of about 1.6 KB is Loki's 64 MB/s ingestion limit, as configured here. Vector retried, and Loki holds every event of the step.
- **48,000/s**: both aggregators were OOM-killed at 22:22:44 (seen by the run's watch on pod restarts; the pods were replaced by the config rollout after the run, so their status no longer shows it). Memory was 937 and 984 MiB of their 1 GiB just before. The Kafka sources fetch faster than the transforms drain, and that queue lives in memory. After the restart each pod re-read events since its last offset commit (every 5 s), and dedupe's cache, which is in memory per pod, was empty: Loki holds 143,199 duplicates from that step, within the up to 240,000 two pods re-reading 5 s at 24,000/s each could produce. Counts can't show whether a loss hides under the duplicates; deduplicating by event ID is M4.2's job (#23).
- **Vector's own counts** are short in the whole-run table (consumed 6,167 fewer than acknowledged) because a killed pod's last increments are never scraped. Loki's per-step count is the reliable one.
- **Kafka lag** reads 4–5 s of traffic at every rate, while p50 latency is 12 ms at 3,000/s: it's measured against offsets committed every 5 s, not the consumer's position.
- **Memory** is each aggregator's working set at the end of the step. **CPU** is cores, from cAdvisor. Kafka (0.1 cores) and Silo (0.06) were never close to a limit; Loki used 1.7 cores at 40,000/s.

## Latency: the knee

Up to 3,000 events/s, p99 is 29 ms, as in the M1 baseline: the producer's 20 ms linger, Kafka, and the transforms. At 4,000/s p99 jumps to 544 ms with p50 unchanged. In an earlier run at 6,000/s, only 62% of events took under 30 ms and 10% took 0.5–1 s: a second mode, not a few outliers.

Vector's own metrics show where the time goes. At 3,000/s the transforms' input queues are close to empty. At 6,000/s they hold about 3 to 11 batches each, rising along the chain from the source (`bench_in_load` 2.7) to the last transform before the sinks (`bench_hot` 10.8); at 16,000/s the source's output queue held 1,373. So events wait inside the aggregator, backed up from the end of the chain. What's ruled out:

- **The sinks' disk buffers**: writes into them took p99 0.5 ms at 3,000/s and under 3 ms at 16,000/s (`vector_buffer_send_duration_seconds`).
- **The latency metric's own path** (`team_latency_events` → the Prometheus exporter): its buffer stayed empty, with sub-millisecond writes.
- **CPU**: each aggregator used about 0.55 cores at 6,000/s, with no CPU limit on a 16-thread host.

The root cause is open. Finding it needs controlled runs (without dedupe, with different Kafka fetch settings, with one sink) and is follow-up work.

## What the runs found and changed

Four runs before this one found problems in the stack and in the measurement. Each is fixed in this change, and the numbers above are from a run with every fix in place.

| Run | Found | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (1–12 k/s, Loki's stock limits) | **The hot path capped at ~2,700 events/s**: Loki answered 70% of pushes with 429. Its default limits are 3 MB/s per stream and 4 MB/s per tenant, and `hot_loki` makes one stream per team and source, so one team can't exceed ~2,000–2,700 events/s into Loki whatever Vector can do. The design held: the 1 GiB disk buffer filled, backpressure stopped the source, and **all 2,640,000 events reached Loki and cold storage**, with none dropped | `deploy/platform/loki/values.yaml` raises the limits (64 MB/s per tenant and per stream): Spillway's per-team budgets are the rate control, and this Loki has one tenant |
| 2 (3–16 k/s) | The pipeline kept up to 16,000/s. The report undercounted (old counters from run 1 still exported), and failed a step on one noisy lag sample | The report counts each counter from its raw samples and uses a trend, not end-minus-start |
| 3 (1–24 k/s) | Every step sustained to 24,000/s, 23,040,000 events delivered exactly. Loki's memory reached 930 MiB of its 1 GiB | – |
| 4 (24–48 k/s) | **Loki was OOM-killed at 24,000/s, then crash-looped replaying its WAL**: `replay_memory_ceiling` defaulted to 4 GB, above the 1 GiB limit. Aborted | Loki gets 4 GiB and a 2 GB replay ceiling |

Two problems outside the benchmark came up too:

- **Operator: a LogPipeline re-created during a canary can stay `RollingOut` indefinitely** ([#56](https://github.com/Andrew-Hinson/spillway/issues/56)). Deleting a team starts a canary; applying the same spec again during it renders the stable config, and the operator never records the new object as part of it. It happened between runs (the previous run's cleanup and the next run's apply). Fixed since: the operator records the pipelines in the stable config whenever they change, not only with a new config.
- **Removing a team gives 7 `reader_failed` errors** on the aggregator that keeps it a moment longer, as its Kafka consumer group rebalances. They come after the drain and aren't counted above.

## Measuring Vector under load

What the report does, and why, since each was wrong at first:

- **Buffer depth** is `vector_buffer_size_events`; `vector_buffer_events` doesn't exist in Vector 0.58, and a query for it reads 0 (an empty buffer).
- **Throttling** doesn't show in `vector_component_errors_total`: a sink retries a 429. The report reads `vector_http_client_responses_total` by status.
- **Counters per Kafka partition** appear with their first event, already counting; **idle components' series expire**, and **a removed component's counter is exported a while longer**. So `increase()` extrapolates, a value at the end misses expired series, and end-minus-start subtracts a previous run. The report walks each series' raw samples from its value at the start (0 if absent) and treats a drop as a restart.
- **A histogram whose flow stalls** (backpressure) is expired and later republished from a different base, so `increase()` over it is meaningless: at 12,000/s in run 1, it reported 846,755 latency samples for a window in which the aggregators consumed about 143,000 events. The report shows no latency for a step with a reset.
- **Lag** is against committed offsets (every 5 s), and briefly reads negative.

## How to reproduce

```bash
make up
make bench STEPS=1000,2000,3000,4000,6000,8000,12000,16000,24000,32000,40000,48000 STEP=4m
```

`make bench` runs `bench/m4_bench.sh`: it builds the load generator, creates `bench.load` and the `bench` team and waits for the canary to promote it, runs the steps as a Job, waits for the backlog to drain, and prints the report. It deletes the Job, the team and the topic afterwards. The run takes `STEPS × STEP` plus about 5 minutes. Don't run anything CPU-heavy on the host meanwhile: everything, the load generator included, shares its cores.

To report a past run again: `python3 bench/throughput.py --log <its loadgen.log> --drain-end <time>` with Prometheus and Loki port-forwarded to `localhost:9090` and `localhost:3100`. `--drain-end` must be before the run's cleanup, which removes the team's metrics.
